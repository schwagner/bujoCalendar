A5 · 5 mm dot grid · right-hand page

Daily spread stencil, three directions

Each plate is drawn to scale in millimetres over an A5 page. Every slot sits on the 5 mm dot pitch. The dark band at the top is the lip that hooks over the page edge. Gaps in long slots are bridges that hold the stencil together; you close them by hand.

The lip

The lip is a fence on the underside. It drops about 3 mm and butts against the top edge of the whole text block, so it registers every page the same way. This sets vertical position.

Horizontal position comes from the two round registration windows. Slide the stencil left or right until a printed dot sits centred in each window, then draw. With a roller, roll from the foot of the page toward the lip so the stroke pushes the stencil into its seat.

Pen trace
1.4 mm plate, 1.0 mm slots (fits 0.3–0.5 mm fineliners), 2.5 mm bridges, etched labels
Ink roller
0.6 mm plate, 1.3 mm slots with a 45° bevel on the top edge, 1.0 mm bridges, circles and boxes cut as bridged rings, numbers and letters cut through in a stencil face
Spine side
Plate stops 12 mm from the gutter so it lies flat on a hardcover
Grid offset
Assumed dots at x = 4 + 5n, y = 5 + 5n. This will be a parameter in the SCAD.

Decisions before the SCAD

  1. Which plate, or which parts to combine.
  2. Pen, roller, or both from one SCAD file with a mode switch.
  3. Left-hand pages: a mirrored second print, or a lip that drops on both faces so one stencil flips?
  4. Where your notebook's dots actually start from the top and outer edges. One measurement each is enough.
  5. For pen tracing: etched hour numbers and day letters on the top face, or a plain plate?
